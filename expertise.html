Tu's IAG investor relations remit

The work that makes an investor conversation credible.

More than managing introductions, his role brings financial intelligence, preparation and communication discipline to IAG's cross-border engagement.

01 / INTELLIGENCE

Investment story and commercial context

Bring together an accurate understanding of IAG's agricultural businesses, their value drivers and the financial logic behind priority initiatives.

02 / STRUCTURE

Capital requirements and investor fit

Frame funding objectives, possible financing considerations and investor information needs without implying that a transaction or instrument is being offered.

03 / ASSURANCE

Risk, governance and diligence

Map operating exposures, documentation expectations, regulatory considerations, decision ownership and points needing specialist review.

04 / COMMUNICATION

Institutional and strategic engagement

Create a professional, consistent experience across discussions, responses, meetings and stakeholder follow-up.

05 / STEWARDSHIP

Continuity after the first conversation

Keep information quality, responsible updates and mutual understanding central to an ongoing relationship.

Financial judgment / IAG context

Capital strategy has to reflect operating reality.

An agriculture-focused investment narrative needs to account for the way risks, costs and cash cycles behave across interconnected businesses.

Financial dimensionWhat warrants scrutinyWhy it matters to investors
Working capitalInventory duration, customer terms and payable cyclesCash discipline can matter as much as revenue growth.
Operating marginsProduct mix, logistics, procurement and input costsMargins must be understood in their commercial context.
Capital allocationFunding priorities, investment timing and use-of-capital logicInvestors want to understand priorities and trade-offs.
Risk managementCommodity pricing, FX, regulation and counterparty exposureThe credibility of assumptions depends on the treatment of risk.
Governance and reportingApproval processes, document quality and consistencyConfidence is strengthened by accountable information.

Information architecture

What investors need is
an organised picture.

A clear hierarchy of information reduces friction while leaving room for scrutiny and independent diligence.

01

Strategic narrative

Purpose, business priorities, markets, operating model and growth assumptions.

02

Financial context

Historical information, forecasts where appropriate, and carefully qualified assumptions.

03

Risk & governance

Oversight, commercial risks, regulatory context and mitigation responsibilities.

04

Operating evidence

Capabilities, milestones, execution constraints and supporting documentation.

05

Transaction context

Proposed structure or objectives, if relevant and authorised for disclosure.

06

Communication discipline

Document versions, contacts, response timelines and an agreed reporting cadence.

Investor-relations design principles

Make every next step
easier to understand.

Good engagement is deliberate, not overcomplicated.

01

Be precise

Separate known facts from plans, assumptions and illustrative scenarios.

02

Be accountable

Keep information and decisions with the right owners.

03

Be consistent

Tell one coherent story across materials and meetings.

04

Be responsive

Close loops and communicate unresolved matters clearly.
